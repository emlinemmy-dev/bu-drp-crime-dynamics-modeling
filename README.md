# Directed Reading Program: Dynamic Systems & Crime Modeling – Boston University

Completed a term-long quantitative modeling fellowship in Boston University's Directed Reading Program (Fall 2025) under the mentorship of Xuan Loc Huynh (PhD candidate in Mathematics). The research focused on analyzing ordinary differential equations (ODEs), evaluating system stability, and replicating dynamic population models.

## Methodology & Core Contributions
* **Mathematical Foundations:** Completed advanced study and problem sets in linear and non-linear dynamic systems covering theoretical stability, phase spaces, and chaotic behavior.
* **Crime-Spread Model Replication:** Replicated the quantitative dynamic model from *Modeling the Underlying Dynamics of the Spread of Crime* (McMillon, Simon, & Morenoff, 2014) incorporating crime contagion variables.
* **Equilibrium & Stability Analysis:** Solved dynamic differential equations using phase portraits and Lyapunov functions to establish stability thresholds and identify conditions for low-crime equilibrium states.
* **Numerical Approximation:** Programmed Python scripts utilizing Euler's Method to execute numerical approximations, demonstrating time-series trajectory predictions for complex dynamic systems.
* **Technical Presentation:** Delivered a technical oral presentation of the mathematical framework, predictive Python scripts, and simulation outputs at the program's final symposium.
